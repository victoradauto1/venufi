# Real World Use Case — Cultural Venue Expansion (Rio de Janeiro)

> This example demonstrates how **VenueFi** can be applied to a real-world business.

## 🏛️ Context

A historic multi-floor building located in Rio de Janeiro operates a highly profitable bar on the ground floor, featuring billiards and karaoke. The venue has a strong local reputation and consistent customer flow.

- **Ground Floor:** Bar (profitable, established)
- **Second Floor:** Underutilized (currently low-performance thrift shop)
- **Third Floor:** Private residence

The building maintains historical characteristics, limiting external modifications but allowing internal redevelopment.

## 💡 Opportunity

The second floor (~100m²) can be transformed into a multi-purpose cultural and event space, leveraging the existing brand and foot traffic of the bar.

**Planned uses:**
- Cultural events (live music, stand-up comedy)
- Private parties and seasonal events
- Corporate gatherings

## 💰 Capital Raise

**Target:** `8 ETH`

**Allocation:**
- Structural and interior renovation: `3 ETH`
- Acoustic insulation: `2 ETH`
- Lighting and stage setup: `1 ETH`
- Furniture and fixtures: `1 ETH`
- Operational buffer: `1 ETH`

## 📈 Revenue Model

**Estimated usage:**
- ~8 events per month
- Average revenue per event: `~0.015 ETH`

**Estimated monthly revenue:** `≈ 0.12 ETH`

### Investor Return Model
- **15%** of gross revenue distributed to investors
- **10%** operator fee (deducted at deposit)
- Remaining revenue retained by the operator

> *Revenue is distributed on-chain using the `accRevenuePerToken` model.*

## 🎯 Why This Works

- **Existing profitable venue** reduces demand risk.
- **Strong brand and customer base** already established.
- **Underutilized asset** creates asymmetric upside.
- **Minimal dependency** on new customer acquisition.

## ⚠️ Risks

- Regulatory constraints (historic building restrictions).
- Noise limitations and neighborhood constraints.
- Event demand variability.
- Execution risk in renovation.
